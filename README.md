# top cashback: how to compare payout minimums, tracking rules and real rates before you sign up

Most "best cashback site" roundups are ranked by what the site pays for the click, not by what it pays you. That's the trap with searching for top cashback: you get the same three names in a different order, and almost nobody mentions the two things that actually decide how much money reaches your bank account — the cash-out threshold, and whether your purchase tracks at all.

Here's the practical version of the question, then: what separates a cashback platform that quietly pays you a few hundred a year from one you forget about after a fortnight?

## Start with the money flow, because everything else follows from it

Cashback portals don't discount anything. You pay the same price at the merchant as anyone else. The retailer pays the portal an affiliate commission for sending you over, and the portal hands part of that commission back to you. That's the entire business model, and it explains all the platform's quirks: why you have to start your session from the portal, why cashback sits "pending" for weeks, and why the rate for the same shop can differ between two platforms on the same day.

ShopBack, the Singapore-founded platform that's been running since 2014, is the same model with a few extras bolted on. It now covers 13 markets and says it works with more than 20,000 merchant partners globally, with over 50 million users [1]. It launched in the US in May 2025 out of Austin, Texas, with more than 2,000 US merchants live at launch, including Amazon, Walmart, Best Buy, Expedia, Ulta, Macy's, Uber and DoorDash [1].

That Amazon line is worth pausing on. Most cashback portals can't pay on Amazon purchases because of Amazon's affiliate terms; ShopBack's US offer was built specifically around earning cashback there, but only through its app or browser extension [1]. The rate and the monthly cap on that have been revised repeatedly — third-party trackers have reported it moving from a flat per-order amount to a percentage with a five-orders-per-month ceiling, and community threads in 2026 report it dropping to near zero for some users [2][3]. Treat any Amazon rate you read anywhere, including here, as a snapshot.

## The six checks that separate a top cashback site from a mediocre one

Before you compare headline percentages, compare these. Rates are the loudest number and the least stable.

**1. Cash-out minimum and how you get paid.** A 15% rate is worthless if the minimum balance is high and the only payout route is one you don't use. ShopBack's US setup pays out from US$5 in confirmed cashback, with no withdrawal fee, via PayPal or ACH transfer to a linked US bank account [4]. Some bonus cashback requires a slightly higher confirmed balance — ShopBack's own support documentation puts that at US$10 confirmed — and there's a US$300 daily withdrawal cap [5].

**2. How long "pending" lasts.** This is the honest bottleneck in every cashback programme. Retail orders typically confirm 30–90 days after purchase, travel bookings after the trip has finished, because the merchant holds the return window open [4]. Platforms that obscure this are the ones generating angry reviews.

**3. Tracking reliability.** Cashback is attribution: the portal has to be the last click before checkout. That's why ShopBack's own guidance is blunt — start every purchase from the app, site or extension, and don't wander off to another tab or browser mid-session. Its browser extension does the remembering for you by detecting when you land on a partner store and offering one-tap activation.

**4. Whether the rate applies to the things you actually buy.** Groceries and streaming rarely go above low single digits anywhere. Travel, fashion and beauty, and app-based promos are where the numbers get interesting. A portal being "top" for someone booking hotels may be useless for someone who mostly buys electronics.

**5. Membership fees.** A free tier plus a paid tier is now standard. Rakuten-style free portals sit next to subscription models, and you want to know the fee before you decide it's worth it.

**6. Signup and referral bonuses — and their conditions.** These are real money, but always conditional. Reported US new-user bonuses have landed in the US$10–US$20 range depending on the market and the code [6][7], while Singapore coverage in late 2026 described a S$10 welcome bonus unlocked by spending at least S$20 through the platform and earning S$10 in confirmed cashback of your own within 180 days [8]. Read the second half of the sentence, not the first.

## What realistic rates look like

ShopBack's cashback across its merchant list generally runs from about 1% up to 20%, with boosted offers going higher for short windows. Reported examples from independent trackers include Best Buy at 0.5% and Shein at up to 12%, plus a recurring monthly travel campaign covering up to 15% on hotels and 11% on flights with partners like Trip.com and Expedia [6]. Boosted offers rotate constantly and the aggressive ones are conditional — a widely-tracked Temu promotion offering 100% cashback to new customers with a cash cap, and 20% for existing ones, has been re-run with different caps several times [12].

For scale rather than hype: ShopBack's own blog publishes a first-person 12-month breakdown totalling about US$620 in cashback on roughly US$12,000 of online spending [13]. That's the brand's own content, so treat it as marketing — but the arithmetic is instructive. Roughly 5% on a normal household's online spend, and the biggest single month was driven by one hotel booking, not by daily grocery orders.

The pattern holds across every cashback platform: large, infrequent purchases where the merchant is paying for new customers are where the money is. Everyday retail is where the pennies are.

## Getting the money out without drama

Three steps, in this order. Wait for cashback to move from pending to confirmed; link PayPal or a US bank account while signed in; request the withdrawal once you clear the minimum [4]. PayPal transfers typically arrive within a few business days, ACH timing depends on your bank, and there's no deadline on a confirmed balance [4]. ShopBack also supports ACH in other markets, with user reports describing it as less smooth than PayPal.

One administrative detail worth knowing if you're in the US: cashback withdrawals can feed into 1099-K reporting thresholds, and some states set that threshold far below the federal figure [3]. Not a reason to skip cashback; a reason not to be surprised by a tax form.

## Five habits that decide whether you actually get paid

- **Always start from the portal.** Direct navigation to a retailer kills attribution, even at shops you've used before. Each session is its own click-through.
- **Install the extension if you shop on desktop.** It converts a habit you have to remember into a badge that appears at checkout. Multiple reviewers describe this as the single change that turned cashback from sporadic to consistent [6][13].
- **Don't stack outside coupon codes.** Using an unrelated coupon site's code can break the commission chain and void the cashback.
- **Screenshot your order confirmations.** Tracking misses happen on every portal. ShopBack handled a reported Temu tracking failure through manual review when the buyer had order details to hand [12].
- **Check the merchant page before buying, not after.** The rate on the merchant page is the one that applies, and boosts expire on countdown timers.

## Who should skip this

If nearly all your spending happens at shops outside a portal's partner list, or if you know you won't reliably click through, the return is close to zero and the subscription tier is definitely not worth it. Same if you're an infrequent online shopper who'd rather not think about it.

If you do buy online regularly — travel, fashion, home goods, food delivery, the occasional big-ticket item — the setup cost is one signup and one extension install, and everything after that is a habit.
